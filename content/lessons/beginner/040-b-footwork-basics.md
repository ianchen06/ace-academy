Tennis is a running sport interrupted by swings. Beginners almost always practise the swing and never the running, which is why their good shots only happen when the ball comes straight to them. Footwork is what makes a good swing available on every ball, not just the convenient ones.

There are four movements to learn. The side shuffle keeps your shoulders facing the net for short adjustments. The crossover step covers distance — the back foot crosses in front and you run. The adjustment step is a small stutter step taken close to the ball to fine-tune your distance. The recovery step brings you back toward the middle after the shot.

Use the shuffle inside about two steps of distance and the crossover beyond it. Trying to shuffle to a wide ball is the most common beginner movement error: it is slow, and you arrive late and off balance.

The last three steps before contact decide the shot. Big steps get you to the neighbourhood; small adjustment steps put you at the right distance from the ball — roughly an arm plus racquet away, with the ball beside your front hip. Too close is worse than too far, because you cannot extend through contact.

Balance is the goal of all of it. Knees bent, weight on the balls of your feet, head still and level. If your head bobs up as you swing, you were not balanced, and the strings arrived somewhere other than where you aimed them.

Finally, movement does not end at contact. Recover toward the middle immediately after every shot, split step as your opponent hits, and you are ready for the next ball instead of watching your last one.
