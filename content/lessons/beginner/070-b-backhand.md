Beginners often start with a two-handed backhand: dominant hand low on the grip, non-dominant hand above it, both hands working together like a left-handed forehand (for a right-handed player) with support from the top hand.

The one-handed backhand uses an Eastern or Continental backhand grip, a bigger shoulder turn, and a straighter arm through contact. It takes longer to groove but offers more reach and slice options later on.

Whichever you choose, the keys are the same: turn your shoulders early, keep the racquet head above your wrist through contact, and transfer weight forward into the shot.
