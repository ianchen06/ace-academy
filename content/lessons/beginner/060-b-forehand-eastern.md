This is the forehand to learn first.
The Eastern grip (base knuckle on bevel 3) gives a square racquet face at a waist-high contact, so the ball goes where the strings point and mistakes are easy to diagnose.
Work through the checkpoints in order, and only add pace once you can hit all eight without thinking.

If you have not settled on a grip yet, read [Choosing Your Forehand Grip](/curriculum/beginner/b-grip-selection) first — the checkpoints below assume an Eastern grip.

### Step 1 — Set the grip and the ready position

Base knuckle on bevel 3, non-dominant hand cradling the racquet throat, racquet out in front, knees soft, weight forward on the balls of your feet.

### Step 2 — Turn as a unit

The instant you read forehand, turn shoulders and hips together, with the non-dominant hand still on the throat doing the turning.
Your chest should end up facing the sideline.
This is one movement, not "racquet back" — taking the racquet back with the arm alone is what produces the arm-only forehand.

### Step 3 — Move with the crossover, arrive with small steps

Cover distance with crossover steps while already turned, then take two or three small adjustment steps so the ball will arrive beside your front hip, an arm plus racquet away.

### Step 4 — Load the outside leg

Bend the outside knee and feel your weight settle into that leg.
This is the coil that the swing unwinds; without it your arm supplies all the power.

### Step 5 — Drop the racquet head below the ball

Let the hand fall so the racquet head is below the height you intend to contact at.
With an Eastern grip this is a shallow drop, roughly a foot below contact, because the swing path is only mildly upward.

### Step 6 — Swing from the ground up

Push off the outside leg, let hips rotate, then shoulders, then the arm — the racquet arrives last.
Keep the wrist relaxed and let it lag rather than steering the racquet with it.

### Step 7 — Contact out in front, beside the front hip

With Eastern, contact is roughly waist height, slightly in front of your front hip, arm comfortably extended but not locked.
The racquet face should be square: if it points at your target at contact, the ball goes there.

### Step 8 — Finish high and hold it

Continue up and across so the racquet ends near your opposite shoulder, and hold that finish for a full second before recovering.
A held finish proves you accelerated through the ball instead of decelerating at it.

## Diagnosing your misses

- **Balls flying long** usually mean the face was open at contact, or you contacted too late.
- **Balls into the net** usually mean the racquet did not drop below the ball, or the head lifted early.
- **Balls sprayed sideways** usually mean contact was behind you — which is a footwork problem, not a swing problem.
