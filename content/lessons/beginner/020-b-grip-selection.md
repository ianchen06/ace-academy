A racquet handle is an octagon: eight flat faces called bevels.
Hold the racquet on edge in front of you and the flat face pointing straight up is bevel 1; numbering continues clockwise for a right-hander (counter-clockwise for a left-hander).
Grips are named by which bevel your index finger's base knuckle rests on, so "one bevel over" is a precise, repeatable instruction rather than a vague feeling.

Continental (base knuckle on bevel 2) is the serve, volley, slice and overhead grip.
It puts the racquet face nearly perpendicular to the ground with a natural high-to-low path, which is why it produces slice easily — and why it is a poor modern forehand grip: you have to contact the ball very close to your body and cannot brush up the back of it comfortably.

Eastern (bevel 3) is the classic "shake hands" forehand grip.
The racquet face sits square to the ball at a comfortable waist-height contact, so it produces a flat, penetrating drive with moderate topspin.
It is the easiest grip to learn a clean contact with, the most forgiving on low balls, and the quickest to change from when you need to slice or volley.

Semi-Western (bevel 4) rotates the hand a further bevel under the handle.
The racquet face is naturally closed, contact moves further in front and higher, and the swing path steepens — this is the default grip of most modern players because it produces heavy topspin without giving up much on flatter drives.

Western (bevel 5) is the extreme end: the palm sits almost under the handle, contact is high and well in front, and the ball comes off with very heavy spin and a high bounce.
It rewards high-bouncing surfaces like clay and punishes low balls, slice and quick grip changes at net.

Pick by the tennis you want to play, not by copying a favourite pro.
If you are learning the game, want quick net transitions, or play on fast low-bouncing courts, start Eastern.
If you want a heavy topspin baseline game and play on slower, higher-bouncing courts, start Semi-Western.
Almost nobody should start at Western — it is a specialist choice you can grow into later, one bevel at a time.
