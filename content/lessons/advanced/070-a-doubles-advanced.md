  - Switch to Australian formation specifically against returners who love to rip crosscourt returns at your net partner.

Poaching — the net player crossing to intercept a crosscourt shot — pressures opponents and creates easy volleys, but must be balanced with fake poaches (bluffing without moving) to keep opponents honest.

Hand signals behind the back before serving (fist = stay, open hand = poach) let the server's partner communicate their intention to the server without tipping off opponents.

The Australian (I) formation — server's partner stationed on the same side as the server rather than the opposite box — takes away the returner's favorite crosscourt return and forces them down the line.

Two-back formation (both players at the baseline) is a defensive fallback used against a strong net rusher or heavy lob attack, trading offense for court coverage.
