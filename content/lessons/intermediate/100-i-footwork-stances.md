A stance is not a style choice, it is a response to how much time you have and where the ball is.
Intermediate players usually own one stance and force every ball into it — stepping across on a wide ball that needed an open stance, or standing open on a short ball that begged to be stepped into.

Neutral (closed) stance: front foot steps across toward the net, shoulders perpendicular to the baseline.
Use it when you have time and can move forward into the ball — short balls, approach shots, second-serve returns you are attacking.
It transfers weight forward better than any other stance and produces the most penetrating ball.

Semi-open stance: back foot planted, front foot angled toward the net post rather than stepped fully across.
This is the workhorse of modern rally tennis: it allows a full hip and shoulder rotation, some forward weight transfer, and quick recovery.
Use it on the majority of balls hit within a couple of steps of your position.

Open stance: both feet roughly parallel to the baseline, weight loaded onto the outside leg.
Use it when the ball pulls you wide or arrives fast.
Power comes from rotation and from the ground through the outside leg rather than from stepping forward, and crucially the loaded outside leg is already positioned to push you back toward the middle.

The loading pattern is the same in all three: weight settles into the leg furthest from the target, the knee bends, and the leg then drives up and around as the hips rotate.
What changes is which leg does it and how much of the energy goes forward versus rotational.

On the run, add the two specialist steps.
The hop or "jump" recovery after an open-stance forehand converts rotational energy into a first recovery step.
And on a ball hit deep behind you, drop-step with the back foot first rather than backpedalling — backpedalling is slow and it is how people fall over.

Choosing under pressure is a recognition skill, not a thinking skill.
Feed yourself the three ball types repeatedly until stance selection becomes automatic, because in a rally there is no time to decide.
